# Marketing on the Move: Decoding STP in the Real World

A field-based brand positioning audit of **Forest Essentials**, **Kama Ayurveda** and **Puraveda**, three competing brands in Ayurvedic beauty and personal care.

Done as a mall immersion assignment for *Marketing in Practice* (PGDM, Fortune Institute of International Business, New Delhi).

## Overview

Brands say a lot about how they want to be seen. This project checks what actually reaches the customer at the shelf.

We visited the mall on 18 August 2026 and observed all three brands in person. Forest Essentials and Kama Ayurveda were seen in their dedicated stores. Puraveda was seen at a kiosk inside Tira Beauty Luxe. We then built an STP (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning) analysis from what we saw and heard, and compared it with an AI-generated STP analysis.

**Mall visited:** DLF Promenade / Ambience Mall, Vasant Kunj, New Delhi
**Date of visit:** 18 August 2026

## Methodology

Each brand was observed on the same five parameters:

1. Price architecture
2. Store design and atmospherics
3. Customer profile
4. Brand communication cues
5. Salesperson interaction

Observation ran for about 10 minutes per brand. Where field notes were missing, we reported that directly instead of filling the gap with assumptions. For example, no customers were present at the Puraveda kiosk during the observation, so we made no claim about its customer demand.

## Project structure

| Phase | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Phase 1** | Field observation and retail audit for each brand |
| **Phase 2** | STP analysis (segmentation, targeting, positioning statement) and a cross-brand comparison |
| **Consumer behaviour lens** | Cultural, Social, Personal and Psychological factors applied to the findings |
| **Phase 3** | AI-generated STP, a reality check against field evidence, and an AI usage log |
| **Field evidence** | Photos from the visit |

## Key findings

| | Forest Essentials | Kama Ayurveda | Puraveda |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Price impression** | Premium | Premium | Mid-range |
| **Observed price range** | ₹500 to ₹7,800 | ₹795 to ₹9,495 | Not fully recorded |
| **Format** | Dedicated store | Dedicated store | Kiosk in Tira Beauty Luxe |
| **Store feel** | Luxury, calm, fresh | Earthy, traditional | Modern beauty environment |
| **Customer age** | 30 to 40 and 40+ | Around 30 to 40 (salesperson: 30+) | Salesperson: 35+ |
| **Position** | Luxury Ayurveda | Authentic Ayurveda | Modern, mid-range Ayurveda |

- **Forest Essentials** had the sharpest position. Warm lighting, fragrance, sofa seating, personalised wrapping and staff presentation all supported the same luxury message.
- **Kama Ayurveda** had a clear authenticity message, backed by traditional store cues and the salesperson's line that Kama is "more authentic".
- **Puraveda** had a modern, mid-range direction, but it was less visible because of the kiosk format and the short interaction.

## What AI got right, and what it missed

AI correctly identified the broad image of each brand: luxury, authentic, and modern. It could not know the store atmosphere, the actual customers present, how staff behaved, or what salespeople said. Those details only came from being there.

The takeaway is that AI is a useful starting point, but STP work should stay tied to primary evidence. Every use of AI is logged in the report, with what was accepted and what was rejected.

## Limitations

- Observation was short (about 10 minutes per brand) at a single mall on a single day.
- The customer counts show who was present during the visit, not the full customer base.
- Puraveda's price range was not fully recorded, and its salesperson comparison wording was not fully captured.
- Findings describe what we observed, not the brands' wider performance.


## Note on AI use

AI was used for the assignment's required STP comparison and to help organise field notes. All observations, quotes and conclusions come from the field visit.
